## Introduction

Many readers assume that grouping words into phrases and [skimming]({{ 'skimming/' | relative_url }}) are simply two versions of reading faster. In [practice]({{ 'practice/' | relative_url }}), they are different strategies with different goals. Phrase reading aims to preserve the author's meaning while reducing unnecessary pauses between words. Skimming, by contrast, deliberately sacrifices some detail in order to gain speed. Both can increase reading rate, but they do so through different mechanisms and with different consequences for comprehension. Research on reading fluency consistently treats phrasing as part of skilled reading, whereas skimming is usually described as a selective reading technique used when a reader does not need complete coverage of a text.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.readingrockets.org/reading-101/reading-101-learning-modules/course-modules/fluency" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: readingrockets.org">[Reading Rockets+2Reading Rockets]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">readingrockets.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Reading Rockets Fluency</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Reading RocketsFluency: IntroductionFluency is the ability to read a text accurately, at a good pace, and with proper expression and comp...</span></span></span>

## Are you grouping words or just skimming?

The confusion is understandable because both approaches often look faster from the outside. A reader moving smoothly through a page may appear to be skimming even when they are carefully processing every sentence.

The key distinction is simple:

* **Grouping words** means reading all the words while processing them as meaningful units.
* **Skimming** means intentionally reading only part of the text or giving some parts much less attention.

Consider the sentence:

> The committee reviewed the proposal after several weeks of discussion.

A phrase reader may naturally process it as:

> The committee reviewed the proposal / after several weeks of discussion.

Nothing is omitted. The words are simply organised into larger language units.

A skimming reader, however, might focus on only a few elements:

> committee... proposal... weeks of discussion

The general idea remains, but much of the sentence structure and nuance disappears. This difference explains why fluent readers can often read faster without losing understanding, while skimming usually involves accepting a reduction in detail.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://keystoliteracy.com/blog/the-importance-of-teaching-prosody-as-part-of-reading-fluency/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: keystoliteracy.com">[Keys to Literacy+2Sarah&#x27;s Teaching Snippets]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">keystoliteracy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Keys to LiteracyThe Importance of Teaching Prosody as Part of Reading...11 Jul 2023 — Fluent readers chunk words together in appropriate...</span></span></span>

## What grouping preserves that skimming drops

Phrase reading works because language is naturally organised into chunks. Readers do not normally interpret every word in isolation. Instead, they connect related words into meaningful units that reflect grammar and meaning.

### Sentence structure stays intact

When readers process [phrases]({{ 'phrases/' | relative_url }}), they preserve relationships between ideas. Subjects remain connected to verbs, descriptions remain connected to nouns, and cause-and-effect relationships remain visible.

For example:

> The unexpectedly strong results surprised the research team.

A phrase reader tends to perceive:

> The unexpectedly strong results / surprised the research team.

The structure remains intact, helping comprehension remain accurate.

Skimming often weakens those relationships because attention is directed primarily toward keywords and main ideas rather than the full sentence construction. Educational guidance on skimming explicitly describes it as selective reading focused on major ideas rather than complete detail.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://learningcenter.unc.edu/tips-and-tools/skimming/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: learningcenter.unc.edu">[The Learning Center+2AMLE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">learningcenter.unc.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Learning Center Skimming</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Learning CenterSkimming - The Learning CenterSkimming is a strategic, selective reading method in which you focus on the main ideas o...</span></span></span>

### Prosody and meaning remain connected

Historically, reading fluency research has linked phrasing to prosody—the rhythm and expression that reflect understanding. Fluent readers tend to group words into meaningful phrases because that mirrors how language is spoken and understood. This phrasing helps maintain comprehension, especially in longer or more complex material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://keystoliteracy.com/blog/the-importance-of-teaching-prosody-as-part-of-reading-fluency/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: keystoliteracy.com">[Keys to Literacy+2Reading Rockets]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">keystoliteracy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Keys to LiteracyThe Importance of Teaching Prosody as Part of Reading...11 Jul 2023 — Fluent readers chunk words together in appropriate...</span></span></span>

Skimming does not aim to preserve this deeper structure. Its [purpose]({{ 'purpose/' | relative_url }}) is efficiency, not full reconstruction of the author's intended flow of meaning.

### Important details survive

A small qualifier can completely change a sentence:

> The treatment was effective in most cases.

versus

> The treatment was not effective in most cases.

Phrase reading keeps attention on the entire statement. Skimming increases the risk that qualifying words, limitations, or [exceptions]({{ 'exceptions/' | relative_url }}) receive less attention because the reader is searching for the general message rather than complete accuracy. Research comparing [careful reading]({{ 'careful-reading/' | relative_url }}) with skim-reading consistently finds lower comprehension under skimming conditions, even when reading speed increases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12801452/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC+2Sage Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Reading and skimming clinical information: insights from...by MA Soltan · 2025 · Cited by 1 — Reading times are shorter and comprehen...</span></span></span>

## When faster reading becomes selective sampling

The historical debate around speed reading often blurred the boundary between fluent reading and selective reading. Many speed-reading systems promoted dramatic gains in words per minute, while scientific studies repeatedly found trade-offs between reading speed and comprehension when readers moved beyond the limits of normal fluent reading.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/so-much-to-read-so-little-time-how-do-we-read-and-can-speed-readi/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: profiles.wustl.edu">[WashU Research Profiles]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">profiles.wustl.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WashU Research ProfilesHow do we read, and can speed reading help?by K Rayner · 2016 · Cited by 533 — The research shows that there is a...</span></span></span>

This is where skimming enters the picture.

When readers begin skipping sections, reducing attention to sentences, or focusing mainly on headings and keywords, they are no longer merely reading more fluently. They are sampling the text.

Researchers studying reading and eye movements commonly distinguish ordinary reading from skimming. In skimming, the goal is often to locate information quickly or obtain a broad impression rather than achieve complete understanding. Reading rates can increase substantially, but comprehension tends to decline.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1529100615623267" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.sagepub.com">[Sage Journals+2PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.sagepub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sage Journals So Much to Read, So Little Time</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sage JournalsSo Much to Read, So Little Time - Keith Rayner, Elizabeth...by K Rayner · 2016 · Cited by 531 — Reading can be contrasted w...</span></span></span>

This does not make skimming a poor strategy. It simply means it serves a different purpose.

A student previewing a chapter before class may benefit from skimming. A lawyer reviewing a contract or a scientist evaluating research findings usually cannot afford to replace full reading with selective sampling.

## How eye-movement research clarifies the difference

Eye-tracking studies provide an important reality check. Skilled readers do not absorb entire pages in a glance. Instead, they move through text using a sequence of fixations and rapid eye movements called saccades. Even fast readers operate within a limited perceptual span—the region from which useful visual information can be extracted during a fixation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3075059/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC+2PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCEye movements, the perceptual span, and reading speed</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">by K Rayner · 2010 · Cited by 474 — The main findings were that fast readers (reading at about 330 wpm) had a larger perceptual span t...</span></span></span>

Phrase reading fits this model well. Readers use each fixation efficiently by connecting nearby words into meaningful groups. They are still reading the sentence.

Skimming changes the task itself. Instead of attempting full comprehension, the reader strategically allocates attention to selected parts of the page. That distinction helps explain why phrase reading is often compatible with strong comprehension, while aggressive skimming usually is not.

If the answer is the full meaning, grouping words into phrases is usually the better route. If the answer is only the general message, skimming may be more efficient.

## The practical takeaway

Phrase reading and skimming both increase reading speed, but they achieve that speed in fundamentally different ways. Phrase reading improves efficiency by preserving the sentence and processing language in meaningful chunks. Skimming improves efficiency by reducing how much of the text receives full attention. One is primarily a fluency skill; the other is a selective-reading strategy. Understanding that difference prevents a common misunderstanding in discussions of increasing reading speed: reading faster does not always mean reading less, but skimming almost always does.
