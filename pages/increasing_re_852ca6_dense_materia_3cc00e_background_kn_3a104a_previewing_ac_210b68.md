## Introduction

One of the fastest ways to increase reading speed is not to read faster at all. It is to spend a minute or two previewing a text [before reading]({{ 'before-reading/' | relative_url }}) it closely. A quick scan of headings, [abstracts]({{ 'abstracts/' | relative_url }}), figures, captions, introductions and conclusions can activate relevant knowledge that is already stored in memory, making the full text easier to understand when reading begins. Research on reading comprehension consistently links previewing with the activation of prior knowledge, clearer reading goals and improved comprehension.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://courses.lumenlearning.com/englishcomp1/chapter/previewing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: courses.lumenlearning.com">[Lumen Learning+2EEF]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">courses.lumenlearning.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lumen LearningPreviewing &#124; English Composition 1Previewing is a strategy that readers use to recall prior knowledge, set a purpose for re...</span></span></span>

## What to Scan Before Detailed Reading

Previewing is not the same as [skimming]({{ 'skimming/' | relative_url }}) the entire document. The goal is not to understand everything. The goal is to identify enough signals to wake up relevant knowledge before deep reading begins.

For most non-fiction texts, the highest-value elements to scan are:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--checklist" markdown="1">

* The title and subtitle
* Section headings and subheadings<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://info.mheducation.com/rs/128-SJW-347/images/Hoeffner_SE_Annotated_Teacher_Chapter_3.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: info.mheducation.com">[info.mheducation.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">info.mheducation.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Consider the textbook chapter headings in the...Read more...</span></span></span>
* Abstract or executive summary
* Introduction and conclusion
* Figures, charts and diagrams
* Captions and figure labels
* Highlighted terms, bold text or definitions
* Review questions or summary boxes, if present

</div>

These elements reveal how information is organised and what topics are likely to matter most. Educational research on text structure shows that awareness of organisational patterns improves comprehension because readers can fit new information into an anticipated framework rather than building understanding from scratch.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ila.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/rrq.311" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ila.onlinelibrary.wiley.com">[ILA+2Reading Rockets]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ila.onlinelibrary.wiley.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A Meta‐Analysis on the Effects of Text Structure Instruction on...17 Apr 2020 — Findings show that text structure instruction had pos...</span></span></span>

A reader who spends two minutes looking at the abstract, section headings and figures may already know whether the study concerns an experiment, survey or observational analysis. They can often identify the main variables, the general findings and the paper's structure before reading a single detailed paragraph. When detailed reading begins, fewer cognitive resources are spent on orientation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://info.mheducation.com/rs/128-SJW-347/images/Hoeffner_SE_Annotated_Teacher_Chapter_3.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: info.mheducation.com">[info.mheducation.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">info.mheducation.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Consider the textbook chapter headings in the...Read more...</span></span></span>

## How Previewing Prepares Prior Knowledge

The main value of previewing is not information gathering. It is mental preparation.

Cognitive theories of reading often describe knowledge as organised into schemas: networks of related concepts, experiences and expectations. When a reader encounters clues about a topic before reading, relevant schemas become more accessible and easier to use during comprehension.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.edutopia.org/article/activating-students-prior-knowledge/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: edutopia.org">[Edutopia+2Structural Learning]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">edutopia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">activating students prior knowledge</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">12 Ways to Activate Your Students&#x27; Prior Knowledge13 Feb 2026 — “Assigning a perspective from which a text should be read results...</span></span></span>

For example, imagine opening a chapter titled "Causes of Inflation". Before reading, a quick look at headings might reveal sections on monetary policy, supply shocks and consumer demand. Even if the reader has not studied economics recently, those headings may trigger memories from previous courses, news articles or conversations. The text no longer arrives as completely unfamiliar information.

Several educational sources describe previewing as a method for activating prior knowledge before reading. Rather than entering a text cold, readers begin with a rough mental map that guides interpretation and helps them connect new information to existing understanding.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://teaching.vt.edu/teachingresources/adjustinginstruction/priorknowledge.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: teaching.vt.edu">[Read Naturally+3Teaching Excellence+3Lumen Learning]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">teaching.vt.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Teaching ExcellenceActivating Prior KnowledgePower previewing is an effective reading strategy that can activate prior knowledge. This activation process has practical consequences:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Important concepts are recognised more quickly.
* Relationships between ideas become easier to spot.
* Working memory is used more efficiently.
* Fewer rereads are needed to understand difficult sections.

## Why Figures and Diagrams Matter More Than Many Readers Realise

Readers often focus exclusively on headings during previewing and ignore visual elements. This is a missed opportunity.

Figures, charts and diagrams frequently provide the most condensed representation of a text's key ideas. In [technical]({{ 'technical-texts/' | relative_url }}), scientific and business documents, a single figure can reveal relationships that would otherwise require several pages of explanation.

A preview of visual elements can answer questions such as:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-stack" markdown="1">

* What variables are being compared?
* What process is being described?
* What outcome matters most?
* What evidence supports the argument?

</div>

When readers later encounter detailed explanations, they already possess a basic conceptual framework. The text fills in details rather than constructing understanding from nothing.

This is particularly useful in unfamiliar domains. Even when specialised terminology remains unclear, visual structures often provide enough context to activate related knowledge and reduce confusion during detailed reading.

### Step 4: Set a Reading Purpose

Decide what you are looking for before reading closely.

* Understand the main argument.
* Learn the method.
* Evaluate the evidence.
* Find practical recommendations.

## Common Mistakes When Previewing

Many readers either skip previewing entirely or turn it into a lengthy exercise that delays reading.

Three mistakes are especially common.

**Trying to understand everything during the preview.** The objective is orientation, not mastery. Detailed analysis comes later.

**Spending too long on unfamiliar terminology.** A preview should identify important terms, not require immediate research into every one of them.

**Treating previewing as separate from comprehension.** Previewing works because it prepares comprehension. If the preview never activates prior knowledge or establishes a purpose, much of its value is lost.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://courses.lumenlearning.com/englishcomp1/chapter/previewing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: courses.lumenlearning.com">[Lumen Learning+2Kirkwood Pressbooks]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">courses.lumenlearning.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lumen LearningPreviewing &#124; English Composition 1Previewing is a strategy that readers use to recall prior knowledge, set a purpose for re...</span></span></span>

## The Small Investment That Makes Dense Texts Feel Easier

Readers often believe that reading speed begins with the first sentence. In [practice]({{ 'practice/' | relative_url }}), speed often begins before reading starts.

A brief preview helps readers recognise structure, activate relevant knowledge and establish expectations about what they are about to encounter. The time investment is small—often one to three minutes—but it can significantly reduce the effort required to understand dense material. Rather than accelerating the eyes, previewing accelerates understanding, which is the form of speed that matters most when reading for learning.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://courses.lumenlearning.com/englishcomp1/chapter/previewing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: courses.lumenlearning.com">[AMLE+3Lumen Learning+3Teaching Excellence]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">courses.lumenlearning.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lumen LearningPreviewing &#124; English Composition 1Previewing is a strategy that readers use to recall prior knowledge, set a purpose for re...</span></span></span>
