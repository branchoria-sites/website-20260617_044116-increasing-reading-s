## Introduction

One of the fastest ways to spot hidden contract risk is to stop treating the definitions section as administrative boilerplate. In many agreements, the real scope of a party’s obligations is not created by the operative clause itself but by the meaning assigned to a handful of capitalised terms. A confidentiality obligation, indemnity, service commitment, or licence grant can become dramatically broader because a defined term quietly expands what the clause covers. Defined terms often override ordinary language, meaning that the contract’s private dictionary controls the rest of the document.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://legalvision.com.au/what-are-defined-terms-in-contracts/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: legalvision.com.au">[LegalVision+2Maddin Hauser]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">legalvision.com.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">what are defined terms in contracts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">What are Defined Terms in Contracts?15 Aug 2025 — Defined terms in a contract are capitalised words given a specific meaning w...</span></span></span>

For readers trying to increase contract-reading speed, this creates an important shortcut: before analysing a clause in detail, check the definitions that power it. A few minutes spent on definitions can explain obligations that would otherwise require rereading multiple sections.

## Capitalised terms that deserve immediate checking

Not every defined term deserves equal attention. Certain categories routinely determine how much risk a contract transfers.

Terms such as **Services**, **Deliverables**, **Confidential Information**, **Losses**, **Claims**, **Affiliate**, **Intellectual Property**, and **Control** often appear harmless because they resemble ordinary business language. However, once capitalised and defined, they may carry meanings far broader than their everyday use.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://legalvision.com.au/what-are-defined-terms-in-contracts/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: legalvision.com.au">[LegalVision]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">legalvision.com.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">what are defined terms in contracts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">What are Defined Terms in Contracts?15 Aug 2025 — Defined terms in a contract are capitalised words given a specific meaning w...</span></span></span>

A useful [speed-reading]({{ 'myths/' | relative_url }}) technique is to flag any capitalised term that appears in a key obligation and immediately answer three questions:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* What does the definition include?
* What does it expressly exclude?
* Does the definition refer to other defined terms?

</div>

The third question matters because contracts frequently build definitions on top of one another. A broad definition of "Affiliate" can expand a confidentiality obligation, which then expands an indemnity, which then increases liability [exposure]({{ 'exposure/' | relative_url }}) elsewhere in the agreement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.commercii.co.za/defining-affiliate-in-contracts/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commercii.co.za">[Centre for Innovative Commerce CC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commercii.co.za</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centre for Innovative Commerce CCDefining &quot;Affiliate&quot; in Contracts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centre for Innovative...January 2, 2026 — 2 Jan 2026 — Standard wording: “Affiliate means any entity that directly or indirectly control...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 2, 2026</span></span></span>

## Includes, excludes, and repeated uses

The most common expansion mechanism is the word **includes**.

In ordinary conversation, people often assume that a list defines a category. Contract drafting frequently works the opposite way. Terms such as "including", "including but not limited to", and "without [limitation]({{ 'reading-limits/' | relative_url }})" are commonly used to signal that examples are illustrative rather than exhaustive. As a result, the listed items do not restrict the broader category that precedes them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.adamsdrafting.com/including-without-limitation/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: adamsdrafting.com">[Adams on Contract Drafting+2Practical Law]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">adamsdrafting.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Adams on Contract Drafting“Including Without Limitation”</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Here&#x27;s how Black&#x27;s Law Dictionary defines include.Read more...</span></span></span>

Consider a definition stating:

> Confidential Information includes customer data, pricing information, software code, marketing plans, and business forecasts.

A casual reader may focus on the listed items. Legally, the definition may be much broader, because the list serves only as examples of information already captured by the opening language. Sample confidentiality definitions commonly extend protection to virtually any non-public business information and sometimes information belonging to affiliates or customers as well.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://afterpattern.com/clauses/definition-of-confidential-information" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: afterpattern.com">[Afterpattern]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">afterpattern.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Definition of confidential information clause samplesYesterday — “Confidential Information” means any information, technical...</span></span></span>

The opposite expansion technique appears when a definition uses both **means** and **includes**. In legal drafting, "means" typically establishes a core definition, while "includes" enlarges it further. A reader who notices only the initial wording may miss the additional categories added later in the definition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lexisnexis.com/en-gb/legal/glossary/means-includes" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lexisnexis.com">[LexisNexis]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lexisnexis.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Means and includes Meaning &#124; Legal GlossaryIn legal drafting, “means and includes” signals that a defined term has a fixed core...</span></span></span>

Repeated use is equally important. A definition that appears modest in isolation may occur dozens of times throughout an agreement. Contract drafting specialists regularly emphasise that defined terms are intended to operate consistently across the document, which means one broad definition can reshape numerous unrelated clauses simultaneously.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://gowlingwlg.com/en/insights-resources/articles/2017/ensure-that-defined-terms-are-used-consistently-a" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gowlingwlg.com">[Gowling WLG+2Morgan Lewis]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gowlingwlg.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gowling WLGEnsure that defined terms are used consistently; ambiguity...11 Jul 2017 — Defined terms can greatly simplify contract wordin...</span></span></span>

## How broad definitions reshape later clauses

### Confidential information that becomes almost everything

Confidentiality clauses often look balanced when read alone. The hidden issue is usually the definition of confidential information.

Many agreements define confidential information to include [technical]({{ 'technical-texts/' | relative_url }}) information, business plans, customer information, financial data, know-how, future projects, and information that a reasonable person would regard as confidential. Some definitions also extend protection to information received from affiliates, suppliers, or customers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://afterpattern.com/clauses/definition-of-confidential-information" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: afterpattern.com">[Afterpattern]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">afterpattern.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Definition of confidential information clause samplesYesterday — “Confidential Information” means any information, technical...</span></span></span>

The practical effect is that a clause appearing to protect trade secrets may instead govern nearly every non-public communication exchanged during the relationship.

### Affiliates that multiply the number of protected parties

The definition of **Affiliate** is another common source of hidden expansion.

A standard affiliate definition often covers entities that directly or indirectly control, are controlled by, or are under common control with a party. In large corporate groups, that wording can extend rights and obligations far beyond the company named on the signature page.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://weagree.com/clm/contracts/contract-wording/subsidiaries-and-affiliates/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: weagree.com">[weagree.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">weagree.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Subsidiaries and Affiliates</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Contract wording - WeagreeAffiliate means, in relation to a person, any company or other entity, whether or not with legal personality, w...</span></span></span>

For example, a licence granted to a company and its affiliates may effectively extend to numerous subsidiaries. Likewise, confidentiality obligations owed to one contracting party may become obligations owed to a much larger corporate family. Contract commentators frequently warn that broad affiliate definitions can introduce unexpected liabilities and operational burdens if their scope is not carefully reviewed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://itlawco.com/affiliates-in-contracts-navigating-risks-and-crafting-clarity/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: itlawco.com">[ITLawCo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">itlawco.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">affiliates in contracts navigating risks and crafting clarity</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Affiliates in contracts: Navigating risks and crafting clarity28 Oct 2024 — Affiliates in contracts: avoid pitfalls, manage risks...</span></span></span>

### Losses and claims that widen indemnity exposure

Indemnity clauses often depend on defined terms such as **Losses**, **Damages**, or **Claims**.

A reader may assume liability covers direct financial harm. The definition may instead include legal fees, investigation costs, penalties, settlements, administrative expenses, and other categories of loss. Once those items are embedded in the definition, the indemnity obligation automatically expands without any obvious change to the indemnity clause itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/350868/000035086820000014/iti-2020127xexhibit101.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sec.gov">[Securities and Exchange Commission]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sec.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">iti 2020127xexhibit101</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">urities and Exchange Commissioniti-2020127xexhibit101“Confidentiality Agreement” has the meaning set forth in Section 5.14 “Contract”...</span></span></span>

This is why experienced contract reviewers often read indemnity definitions before analysing indemnity wording.

## The fastest review method for definitions

For speed-focused contract reading, definitions should not be read linearly from beginning to end. A more efficient approach is targeted review.

When encountering a major obligation, pause and inspect the definitions behind the key capitalised terms. Pay particular attention to:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* Terms followed by long lists after the word "including".
* Definitions containing "means and includes".
* References to affiliates, successors, subcontractors, or customers.
* Definitions that incorporate other defined terms.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://weagree.com/clm/contracts/contract-clauses-explained/definitions-in-contracts-22-best-practice-rules/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: weagree.com">[weagree.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">weagree.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Definitions in contracts – 22 best practice rules</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">At the same time, it is a contract drafting discipline.Read more...</span></span></span>
* Definitions appearing simple but used repeatedly throughout the agreement.

</div>

This approach reduces the need to reread entire sections because it identifies the mechanism that often drives hidden risk: a small definition that silently changes the meaning of everything built upon it.
