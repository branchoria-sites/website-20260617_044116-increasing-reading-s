## Introduction

When your [purpose]({{ 'purpose/' | relative_url }}) is to judge evidence rather than absorb a general argument, [skimming]({{ 'skimming/' | relative_url }}) changes dramatically. Instead of following the text from beginning to end, you look for the places where claims are supported, quantified, sourced, and tested. This shift is one of the fastest ways to increase reading speed without sacrificing judgement, because large portions of explanatory, rhetorical, or narrative material can be bypassed while you focus on the sections most likely to reveal whether a conclusion is trustworthy. Research on skimming and scholarly reading shows that experienced readers often direct attention towards figures, tables, results, and other high-information sections rather than reading every paragraph in sequence. The key question becomes: where is the evidence actually located?

## Where Should You Look When Evidence Matters Most?

An evidence-focused skim follows the trail of support rather than the trail of storytelling.

In many articles, [reports]({{ 'reports/' | relative_url }}), and research papers, the conclusion appears near the beginning and the end. Evidence, however, is often concentrated in a much smaller set of locations. If your goal is to decide whether a source deserves deeper reading, these areas usually provide the highest return on attention.

A practical [scanning]({{ 'scanning-vs-reading/' | relative_url }}) order is:

1. Main claim or conclusion.

## Results Tables and Figures Often Reveal More Than Pages of Text

Tables, charts, graphs, and summary figures are frequently the densest evidence containers in a document.

A well-designed table can show sample sizes, comparisons, effect sizes, trends, and exceptions in seconds. Likewise, a graph may reveal whether an apparent finding is dramatic, modest, inconsistent, or barely visible. This is why many experienced researchers examine figures [before reading]({{ 'before-reading/' | relative_url }}) large sections of prose.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://x.com/acagamic/status/1752371021523333218?lang=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: x.com">[X (formerly Twitter)+2TUS Library Guides]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">x.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">X (formerly Twitter)How to Better Skim Academic Papers...• Figures • Tables • Graphs Figures, tables, and graphs... show the researchers...</span></span></span>

When skimming for evidence, look for:

* Numerical comparisons rather than descriptive claims.
* Sample sizes and participant counts.
* Error bars, confidence intervals, or uncertainty measures.
* Patterns that match—or contradict—the author's conclusions.
* Negative or mixed findings alongside positive ones.

Results sections are particularly valuable because their purpose is to present what was found rather than explain what those findings mean. That separation can help readers distinguish evidence from interpretation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sjsu.edu/writingcenter/docs/handouts/Results%20Section%20for%20Research%20Papers.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sjsu.edu">[San José State University+2Wordvice]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sjsu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">San José State University Results Section for Research Papers</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">San José State UniversityResults Section for Research PapersAugust 16, 2022 — by C Ammon · Cited by 5 — The results section should presen...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 16, 2022</span></span></span>

A useful habit is to ask: "If I removed the author's explanation, what would these numbers or figures still show?"

    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();

        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');

        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);

          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }

        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });

## References Can Be Read as Evidence About the Evidence

Evidence-focused skimming does not stop at the findings themselves. It also examines where those findings came from.

A reference list acts as a credibility signal. Readers do not need to inspect every citation, but a quick scan can reveal important clues:

* Are sources recent or decades old?
* Are they primary studies or repeated citations of reviews and commentaries?
* Do references come from recognised journals, institutions, or organisations?
* Is there a diversity of sources or reliance on a narrow group?

Even a brief inspection can reveal whether an article is grounded in substantial research or largely supported by opinion and repetition.

For non-academic writing, the same principle applies. Look for named sources, linked evidence, original datasets, official reports, and transparent attribution. Unsupported assertions often appear confident on the surface but leave little traceable evidence behind.

## Signs of Strong Support Versus Weak Support

Evidence-focused skimming becomes much faster when you learn to recognise common signals of strength and weakness.

### Indicators of stronger support

Strong evidence often displays several of these characteristics:

* Findings are linked to identifiable data.
* Methods or study designs are described clearly.
* Sample sizes are visible.
* Limitations are acknowledged.
* Results include uncertainty measures rather than simple certainty claims.

Weak support often shows different patterns:

* Conclusions appear without visible data.

## A Faster Reading Path for Evidence Seekers

Consider two readers examining a twenty-page report.

The first reads sequentially, hoping to reach a reliable judgement by the end. The second begins with the conclusion, jumps to the figures, checks the evidence tables, scans the references, and only then decides whether the full text deserves careful reading.
