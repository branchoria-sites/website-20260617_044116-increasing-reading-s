## Introduction

One of the least visible but most important costs of RSVP (Rapid Serial Visual Presentation) reading systems is the loss of parafoveal preview. In normal reading, skilled readers do not process only the word they are directly looking at. They also extract information from nearby words that lie just outside the centre of vision, allowing the brain to prepare for what comes next. This advance processing makes reading faster, smoother and less effortful.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22042596/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pub Med Parafoveal processing in reading</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Parafoveal processing in reading - PubMed - NIHby ER Schotter · 2012 · Cited by 846 — The present review summarizes research invest...</span></span></span>

One-word flashing apps remove this capability by displaying only a single word at a time. The result is a trade-off: readers may move through text quickly, but they lose a source of information that normally helps recognition, prediction and comprehension.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1529100615623267" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.sagepub.com">[Sage Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.sagepub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sage JournalsSo Much to Read, So Little Time - Keith Rayner, Elizabeth...by K Rayner · 2016 · Cited by 519 — Another concern about apps...</span></span></span>

## What Parafoveal Preview Does

Parafoveal preview refers to the processing of words before they are directly fixated. While the eyes are focused on one word, information from words to the right (for readers of English) is gathered in lower-resolution [peripheral vision]({{ 'peripheral-vision/' | relative_url }}). Researchers have shown that readers can obtain useful information about upcoming words, including their length, letter patterns and, in some cases, aspects of their likely identity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22042596/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed+2PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pub Med Parafoveal processing in reading</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Parafoveal processing in reading - PubMed - NIHby ER Schotter · 2012 · Cited by 846 — The present review summarizes research invest...</span></span></span>

This preview creates what researchers call a *preview benefit*. When a word has already been partially processed before the eyes land on it, recognition is faster and fixation times are shorter. Studies using [eye-tracking]({{ 'eye-tracking/' | relative_url }}) and gaze-contingent display techniques consistently find that reading becomes less efficient when valid previews are removed or replaced with misleading information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3140553/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC+2PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Parafoveal preview during reading: Effects of sentence positionby SJ White · 2011 · Cited by 21 — Although text in the parafovea is vi...</span></span></span>

The benefit is not a minor optimisation. It is woven into the way experienced readers coordinate attention and eye movements. Reading is faster because the next word often receives a cognitive head start before direct inspection begins.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3633587/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">See Before You Jump: Full Recognition of Parafoveal Words...by PC Gordon · 2012 · Cited by 56 — Parafoveal preview allows lexical pro...</span></span></span>

## Why One-Word Displays Remove It

In conventional text, multiple words are visible simultaneously. Even though only one small region is sharply focused, surrounding words remain available for partial processing. RSVP systems remove that visual neighbourhood entirely.

A reader viewing one word at a time has no opportunity to inspect upcoming text. The next word does not exist on the screen until the software decides to reveal it. As a result, the brain cannot begin preprocessing the upcoming word during the current fixation. The normal overlap between processing the current word and preparing for the next one disappears.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1529100615623267" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.sagepub.com">[Sage Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.sagepub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sage JournalsSo Much to Read, So Little Time - Keith Rayner, Elizabeth...by K Rayner · 2016 · Cited by 519 — Another concern about apps...</span></span></span>

This is fundamentally different from merely changing font size or page layout. The mechanism itself is removed. In normal reading, attention can gradually shift toward an upcoming word before the eyes arrive there. RSVP forces strictly sequential processing: one word must disappear before the next becomes available.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3633587/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">See Before You Jump: Full Recognition of Parafoveal Words...by PC Gordon · 2012 · Cited by 56 — Parafoveal preview allows lexical pro...</span></span></span>

Researchers reviewing [speed-reading]({{ 'myths/' | relative_url }}) applications have highlighted this loss as one of the main reasons why eliminating eye movements does not automatically translate into superior reading performance. Eye movements consume time, but they are coordinated with parafoveal information gathering that contributes directly to reading efficiency.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1529100615623267" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.sagepub.com">[Sage Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.sagepub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sage JournalsSo Much to Read, So Little Time - Keith Rayner, Elizabeth...by K Rayner · 2016 · Cited by 519 — Another concern about apps...</span></span></span>

## How Preview Supports Fluent Reading

The value of parafoveal preview becomes clearer when considering what fluent readers accomplish during each fixation.

Instead of recognising words from scratch, readers often arrive at a word with partial information already available. They may know its approximate length, have processed several letters, or have narrowed down possible lexical candidates. This reduces the work required once the word is directly fixated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mdpi.com/1995-8692/18/2/3" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mdpi.com">[MDPI+2Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mdpi.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Reduced Capacity for Parafoveal Processing (ReCaPP)...by LB Fernandez · 2025 · Cited by 1 — This is the first study not using parafo...</span></span></span>

Preview also helps coordinate eye movements. The reading system continuously decides where to move next and how far to jump. Information gathered from upcoming words assists these decisions, contributing to the characteristic rhythm of skilled reading. Computational and eye-movement research indicates that parafoveal information plays an important role in guiding this process.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-92140-z" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">We present an explicit</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Predictive modeling of parafoveal information processing during...June 21, 2021 — Here, we report results on the use of word infor...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 21, 2021</span></span></span>

Evidence for the importance of preview appears whenever it is experimentally reduced. ## What the Evidence Means for RSVP Reading

The central claim behind many one-word reading systems is that removing eye movements should increase reading speed. Eye-movement research suggests that this view captures only one side of the equation.

Reading speed depends not only on moving the eyes but also on using the time between eye movements productively. Parafoveal preview allows readers to process parts of future words while still dealing with the current one. RSVP eliminates the movement cost, but it also removes this advance processing benefit.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1529100615623267" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.sagepub.com">[Sage Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.sagepub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sage JournalsSo Much to Read, So Little Time - Keith Rayner, Elizabeth...by K Rayner · 2016 · Cited by 519 — Another concern about apps...</span></span></span>
