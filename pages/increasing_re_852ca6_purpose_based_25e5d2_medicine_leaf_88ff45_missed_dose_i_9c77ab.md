## Introduction

When you are reading a medicine leaflet quickly, the section on missed doses is one of the places where you should deliberately [slow down]({{ 'slow-down/' | relative_url }}). Many people assume the advice will always be the same: take the dose when remembered, skip it if the next dose is due soon, and never double up. While that pattern is common, it is not universal. Different medicines have different instructions because the consequences of a missed dose vary enormously. For some medicines, a delay may have little effect. For others, missing a single dose can increase the risk of blood clots, seizures, loss of symptom control, or unintended pregnancy. Regulatory guidance therefore requires leaflets to explain what to do if a dose is forgotten, and those instructions are tailored to the specific medicine.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5fe086c18fa8f5149718d66a/Best_practice_guidance_on_patient_information_leaflets.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: service.gov.uk">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">service.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">• Description ...Read more</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">practice guidance on patient information leaflets (PILs)treatment will last, what to do if a dose is missed and, if relevant, what do in...</span></span></span>

## Why One Missed Dose Can Mean Different Things

The main reason missed-dose advice varies is that medicines behave differently in the body. Factors such as how long a drug remains active, how quickly its effects wear off, and how dangerous a temporary loss of treatment might be all influence the recommendation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://australianprescriber.tg.org.au/articles/ive-missed-a-dose-what-should-i-do.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: australianprescriber.tg.org.au">[Australian Prescriber]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">australianprescriber.tg.org.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Australian Prescriber I&#x27;ve missed a dose; what should I do?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Australian Prescriberby A Gilbert · 2002 · Cited by 16 — If it is almost time for the next dose, skip the missed dose and take the next d...</span></span></span>

A medicine with a long duration of action may continue working even after a dose is missed. By contrast, a medicine that leaves the body quickly can create a meaningful gap in treatment if the next dose is delayed. [Timing]({{ 'timing/' | relative_url }}) also matters because taking two doses too close together may increase side effects or toxicity. This balance between restoring treatment and avoiding overdose is what drives many leaflet instructions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.happyhealthylives.uk/clientfiles/files/Missed%20and%20Delayed%20Doses%20of%20Medication%20in%20Care%20Homes%20July%202022%281%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: happyhealthylives.uk">[Happy Healthy Lives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">happyhealthylives.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Taking subsequent doses too close together can increase the risk of side effects. Research reviewing patient information leaflets found substantial variation in missed-dose advice across medicines and concluded that guidance is often more complex than patients expect. The review also noted that evidence supporting some recommendations is not always clearly presented to patients.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7803707/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCWhat should patients do if they miss a dose?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A systematic...by A Albassam · 2020 · Cited by 29 — Advice on missed doses is generally inadequate. ## Common Missed-Dose Patterns

Although each medicine must be checked individually, several recurring patterns appear in leaflets.

**Take it as soon as remembered.**

Many once-daily or twice-daily medicines use this approach. If enough time remains before the next scheduled dose, patients are often advised to take the forgotten dose immediately and then continue normally.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sps.nhs.uk/articles/advising-on-missed-or-delayed-doses-of-medicines/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sps.nhs.uk">[SPS - Specialist Pharmacy Service]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sps.nhs.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">advising on missed or delayed doses of medicines</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Then continue taking the medicine at the usual time(s). Advice for high-risk medicines.Read more...</span></span></span>

**Skip the missed dose if the next one is near.**

When taking the missed dose would place two doses too close together, leaflets frequently recommend skipping it and resuming the normal schedule. This reduces the risk of excessive drug levels and adverse effects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sps.nhs.uk/articles/advising-on-missed-or-delayed-doses-of-medicines/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sps.nhs.uk">[SPS - Specialist Pharmacy Service]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sps.nhs.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">advising on missed or delayed doses of medicines</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Then continue taking the medicine at the usual time(s). Advice for high-risk medicines.Read more...</span></span></span>

**Do not take a double dose.**<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.nhs.uk/social-care-and-support/practical-tips-if-you-care-for-someone/medicines-tips-for-carers/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nhs.uk">[nhs.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nhs.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Medicines: tips for carers</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Social care and support guidebe aware that if a dose of the medicine is missed, it&#x27;s dangerous to take a double dose later on to make up...</span></span></span>

This is one of the most common safety warnings. NHS guidance and numerous medicine-specific leaflets caution against doubling up to compensate for a forgotten dose because doing so can increase side effects without reliably restoring the intended treatment effect.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nhs.uk/social-care-and-support/practical-tips-if-you-care-for-someone/medicines-tips-for-carers/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nhs.uk">[nhs.uk+2nhs.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nhs.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Medicines: tips for carers</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Social care and support guidebe aware that if a dose of the medicine is missed, it&#x27;s dangerous to take a double dose later on to make up...</span></span></span>

**Special timing windows.**

Some medicines define exact cut-off points. For example, certain anticoagulants specify whether a missed dose can still be taken depending on whether the next scheduled dose is more or less than six or twelve hours away.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nhs.uk/medicines/anticoagulants/dosage/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nhs.uk">[nhs.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nhs.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Anticoagulant medicines</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DosageJune 5, 2018 — If you&#x27;re taking apixaban or dabigatran twice a day and you miss one of your doses, you should take it as soon as yo...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 5, 2018</span></span></span>

The existence of these different patterns is precisely why the leaflet's missed-dose section should not be skimmed.

## High-Risk Medicines With Special Rules

Some medicines deserve particular attention because the risks associated with missed doses are unusually high.

### Anticoagulants

Medicines that reduce blood clotting, such as direct oral anticoagulants, often have carefully defined missed-dose instructions. The goal is to maintain protection against harmful clots while avoiding excessive bleeding. NHS guidance for medicines such as apixaban, dabigatran, and rivaroxaban uses specific time thresholds to determine whether a missed dose should still be taken.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nhs.uk/medicines/anticoagulants/dosage/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nhs.uk">[nhs.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nhs.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Anticoagulant medicines</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DosageJune 5, 2018 — If you&#x27;re taking apixaban or dabigatran twice a day and you miss one of your doses, you should take it as soon as yo...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 5, 2018</span></span></span>

The need for such precision reflects the narrow balance between too little anticoagulation and too much.

### Hormonal Contraception

Combined oral contraceptive pills are a classic example of medicine-specific missed-dose advice. The correct action depends not only on how many pills were missed but also on where the patient is in the pack and the type of pill being used. Additional contraception or emergency contraception may sometimes be required.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nhs.uk/contraception/methods-of-contraception/combined-pill/missed-or-extra-pill/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nhs.uk">[nhs.uk+2CDC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nhs.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">What to do if you miss a combined pill or take an extra oneTalk to a pharmacist to find out what to do, or read the information leaflet t...</span></span></span>

This complexity exists because effectiveness depends on maintaining hormonal [suppression]({{ 'suppression/' | relative_url }}) of ovulation over time.

### Insulin, Parkinson's Medicines, and Epilepsy Treatments

Certain medicines are considered "time critical" because delays can quickly worsen a patient's condition. NHS and emergency medicine guidance highlights insulin, Parkinson's disease treatments, anticoagulants, and epilepsy medicines as examples where missed or delayed doses may lead to serious complications.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hweclinicalguidance.nhs.uk/all-clinical-areas-documents/download?checksum=c410003ef13d451727aeff9082c29a5c&amp;cid=490" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hweclinicalguidance.nhs.uk">[HWE Clinical Guidance]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hweclinicalguidance.nhs.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Immunosuppressant therapy and cancer medicines. • Contact the specialist clinic or prescriber...Read more...</span></span></span>

For these medicines, the consequences of omission may be more significant than for many routine treatments.

### Immunosuppressants and Cancer Medicines

Some specialist medicines require direct advice from a healthcare professional if a dose is missed.
